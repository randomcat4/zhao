   For a given positive integer k ≥ 1, we deﬁne a constant s≤k (G) which is the least

≥ t satisﬁes N k (S) ≥ 1 for some integer k ≥ 1. The other well-known constant η(G) is
nothing but η(G) = s≤exp(G) (G).
   These constants D(G) and η(G) have received a lot of attention (see for instance [1,4,
5,7–9,11,13–15,20,21]). When G is a cyclic group, we have η(G) = |G| and D(G) = |G|.

If G ∼= Cm ⊕ Cn with m|n is an abelian group of rank 2, then it is known that η(G) =
2m + n − 2 as given in [15] and D(G) = m + n − 1.
   When G is of rank ≥ 3, nothing much is known. For any odd prime p, it is known that
η(Cp3 ) ≥ 8p − 7 ([5]) and η(Cp4 ) ≥ 19p − 18 ([4]) and their exact values are still unkonwn.
Recently, Fan, Gao, Wang and Zhong [7] determined the value η(G) for special types of
abelian groups of rank 3. Apart from these results, Schmid and Zhuang [20] proved that
if G is a ﬁnite abelian p-group with D(G) = 2 exp(G) − 1, then η(G) = 2D(G) − exp(G).
Moreover, they conjectured the following.

                                 η(G) = 2D(G) − exp(G).

   The constants s≤k (G) was introduced by Delorme, Ordaz and Quiroz [3]. It is easy

diﬃcult. In 2010, Freeze and Schmid [10] proved that s≤3 (C2r ) = 2r−1 + 1. In 2017,
Wang and Zhao [22] proved that when G = Cm ⊕ Cn , the constant s≤D(G)−k (G) is equal
