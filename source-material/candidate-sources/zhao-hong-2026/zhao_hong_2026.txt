      OF LENGTH NOT EXCEEDING A GIVEN NUMBER
                                            BY

      Abstract. Let G be a finite abelian group and let k be an integer with k ∈ [exp(G),
D(G) − 1], where exp(G) and D(G) are the exponent and the Davenport constant of G,
respectively. Denote by s≤k (G) the smallest positive integer l ∈ N ∪ {+∞} such that each
sequence of length l over G has a nontrivial zero-sum subsequence of length at most k.
We prove that s≤D(G)−2 (G) ≤ D(G) + 2 for all finite non-cyclic abelian groups except for
C23 , C24 and C2 ⊕ C2n . For some classes of p-groups G, we show that s≤k (G) ≤ 2D(G) − k
for k = c1 pt+1 ∈ D(G)+1

    In zero-sum theory, people often consider the following two problems:
    The direct zero-sum problem is to find the smallest integer ℓ ∈ N such
that every sequence S over G of length |S| ≥ ℓ has a zero-sum subsequence
with prescribed length.
    The inverse zero-sum problem asks about the structure of the sequence S
over G without zero-sum subsequences of prescribed length.
    Let L ⊂ N be a nonempty subset and let sL (G) be the smallest inte-
ger ℓ ∈ N ∪ {∞} such that every sequence S over G of length |S| ≥ ℓ
has a zero-sum subsequence T of length |T | ∈ L. Thus D(G) = sN (G),
η(G) = s[1,exp(G)] (G), s(G) = s{exp(G)} (G), and sk exp(G) (G) = s{k exp(G)} (G),
where η(G), the Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv constant s(G), and the generalized
Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv constant sk exp(G) (G) are all classical zero-sum invari-
ants. The invariant sL (G) is also investigated for various other sets L (see
e.g. (15, 17–19, 29, 38). The readers may want to consult one of the surveys
or monographs (14, 20–22, 24).
    In 2001, Delorme, Ordaz and Quiroz [6] systematically investigated the
invariant sL (G) for the case L = [1, k]. We denote
                      s≤k (G) := s[1,k] (G)   for all k ∈ N.
It is easy to see that s≤k (G) = D(G) if k ≥ D(G), s≤k (G) = D(G) + 1 if
k = D(G) − 1, s≤k (G) = η(G) if k = exp(G), and s≤k (G) = ∞ if 1 ≤ k <
exp(G). Thus it suffices to consider s≤k (G) for k ∈ [exp(G), D(G) − 2].
     The invariant s≤k (G) is a variant of the Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv constant
s(G), one of the most investigated invariants in zero-sum theory. For more,
one can see [34]. The invariant s≤k (G) also has a close connection with other
invariants. In [15, 16], Gao et al. studied sk exp(G) (G) and conjectured that
sk exp(G) (G) = s≤k exp(G) (G) + k exp(G) − 1. Bhowmik and Schlage-Puchta [1]
used s≤k (G) to study the Davenport constant D(G). For more results related
to the Davenport constant, one can see [2, 6]. Freeze and Schmid [10] used
s≤k (G) to study the kth Davenport constant Dk (G), which is the smallest
ℓ ∈ N such that every sequence S over G of length |S| ≥ ℓ has k disjoint
nontrivial zero-sum subsequences. In [4], Cohen and Zémor pointed out a
connection between s≤k (G) and coding theory.
     Even though the direct and inverse zero-sum problems of s≤k (G) have
been widely studied, those problems are still widely open. Freeze and
Schmid [10] obtained the exact value s≤3 (C2r ) = 1 + 2r−1 . Lindström [30]
studied s≤4 (C2r ) when r → ∞. In [32], Luo proved that for a finite abelian
p-group G with a large exponent,
                     s≤exp(G) = η(G) = 2D(G) − exp(G).
For rank 2 groups G, Wang and Zhao [39] proved that
            s≤k (G) = 2D(G) − k       when k ∈ [exp(G), D(G) − 1].
