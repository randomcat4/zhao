Short Zero-Sum Sequences Over Abelian
p-Groups of Large Exponent
Sammy Luo
October 3, 2018
Abstract
Let G be a ﬁnite abelian group with exponent n. Let η(G) denote the
smallest integer ℓ such that every sequence over G of length at least ℓ has
a zero-sum subsequence of length at most n. We determine the precise
value of η(G) when G is a p-group whose Davenport constant is at most
2n − 1. This conﬁrms one of the equalities in a conjecture by Schmid and
Zhuang from 2010.
1 Introduction
Throughout this paper, let G be an additive ﬁnite abelian group. By the Fun-
damental Theorem of Finite Abelian Groups, we can uniquely w rite
G ∼
= Cn1 ⊕ · · · ⊕Cnr ,
where 1 < n 1 | · · · |nr. Here r = r(G) is called the rank of G, and nr = exp(G)
is called the exponent of G. The exponent is, for a ﬁnite abelian group, the
largest order attained by an element of G.
We consider sequences of elements of G. A sequence of length k over G is a
tuple S = ( g1, . . . , g k) of k elements of G, where repetition is allowed and the
order of elements is disregarded. Algebraically, we can con sider sequences over G
as elements of the free abelian monoid over G, a viewpoint that facilitates many
algebraic applications. Accordingly, we can write sequenc es in multiplicative
notation; for example, S = g1 ·. . . ·gk. We say that S is a zero-sum sequence
(ZSS) if ∑k
i=1 gi = 0 . A zero-sum sequence of length at most exp(G) is called
a short zero-sum sequence .
One important class of problems on zero-sum sequences asks f or extremal
conditions on a sequence S guaranteeing the containment of a zero-sum sub-
sequence with speciﬁed properties, such as having a length s atisfying various
constraints. The exploration of this class of problems cent ers on a family of
invariants associated with a group G, called the zero-sum invariants sL(G).
Deﬁnition 1. For L ⊆ N+, let sL(G) be the minimum length ℓ ∈ N+ such that
every sequence S over G of length at least ℓ contains a zero-sum subsequence of
some length t ∈ L. Let sL(G) = ∞ if such a length ℓ does not exist.

A few examples of zero-sum invariants are of particular inte rest.
• sN+ (G) = D(G) is called the Davenport constant.
• s{exp(G)} (G) = s(G) is called the Erdős-Ginzburg-Ziv (EGZ) constant .
• s[1,exp(G)](G) = η(G) is called the η-invariant.
The study of these invariants has applications ranging from non-unique fac-
torizations [7, 8] to zero-sum Ramsey theory [11] and ﬁnite g eometry [1].
The precise values of s(G) and η(G) are known only for some very special
types of groups G. For example, for groups of rank at most 2, we have the
following result.
Theorem 1.1 (Geroldinger, Halter-Koch, [7, Theorem 5.8.3]) . Let G = Cn1 ⊕
Cn2 with 1 ≤ n1|n2. Then
s(G) = 2 n1 + 2n2 − 3 and η(G) = 2 n1 + n2 − 2.
In other cases, some bounds on s(G) and η(G) are known. Schmid and
Zhuang [10] obtained the following result for the case of a p-group with D(G) ≤
2 exp(G) − 1. We will call such a group a p-group with large exponent .
Theorem 1.2 (Schmid, Zhuang, [10, Theorem 1.2]) . Let p be an odd prime and
let G be a ﬁnite abelian p-group with D(G) ≤ 2 exp(G) − 1. Then
2D(G) − 1 ≤ η(G) + exp(G) − 1 ≤ s(G) ≤ D(G) + 2 exp(G) − 2.
Note that equality holds everywhere when D(G) = 2 exp( G)− 1. Schmid and
Zhuang conjectured that equality holds between the three le ftmost expressions
without this extra condition.
Conjecture 1.3 (Schmid, Zhuang, [10, Conjecture 4.1]) . Let G be a ﬁnite
abelian p-group with D(G) ≤ 2 exp(G) − 1. Then
2D(G) − 1 = η(G) + exp(G) − 1 = s(G).
Gao, Han, and Zhang made progress on this conjecture in [5], p roving the
ﬁrst equality under additional conditions on the rank and Da venport constant
of G. Their result is stated here.
Theorem 1.4 (Gao, Han, Zhang, [5, Theorem 1.2]) . Let G be a ﬁnite abelian
p-group with exp(G) = n and D(G) ≤ 2n − 1. Write G = Cn ⊕ H, and let
G′ = Can ⊕ H for some a ≥ 1. Then
2D(G′) − 1 = η(G′) + exp(G′) − 1,

Conjecture 1.5 (Gao, [3, Conjecture 2.3]) . For any ﬁnite abelian group G,
η(G) = s(G) − exp(G) + 1.
This conjecture holds for every G for which η(G) and s(G) have been deter-
mined, and is also known to hold in a few other special cases (s ee Theorem 6.6
in [4]).
In this paper, we prove the ﬁrst equality in Conjecture 1.3 in general.
Theorem 1.6. Let G be a ﬁnite abelian p-group with D(G) ≤ 2 exp(G) − 1.
Then
2D(G) − 1 = η(G) + exp(G) − 1.
We also give an extension of Theorem 1.6 to groups of the form G′ = Ca ⊕ G,
where G is a p-group with D(G) ≤ 2 exp(G) − 1 and a is not divisible by p.
In the process of proving Theorem 1.6, we introduce some addi tional zero-
sum invariants and techniques for using them, whose study ma y prove inter-
esting in their own right or when applied to proving the secon d equality in
Conjecture 1.3.
In the next section, we recall some useful previous results o n zero-sum in-
variants in various settings. Afterwards, we give a simple p roof of our main
result and the extension. Finally, we extend the methods we u se to make some
progress on tackling the second half of Conjecture 1.3 and di scuss some ideas
for further work.
2 Background and T ools
We start by giving some bounds on the invariants D(G), η(G), and s(G) for
general G.
Theorem 2.1. For any ﬁnite abelian group G,
D(G) ≤ η(G) ≤ s(G) − exp(G) + 1 ≤ | G|.
This result can be found in Lemma 5.7.2 and Theorem 5.7.4 of [7 ]. Conjec-
ture 1.5 is just the claim that equality always holds in the se cond inequality.
Let D∗ (G) = ∑r
i=1(ni − 1) + 1. A simple construction shows that D∗ (G) ≤
D(G) for any G. We have already seen that equality holds for a group of rank
at most two. Equality also holds for p-groups and in another case of interest.
Theorem 2.2 (Kruyswijk, Olson, [9]) . If G is a p-group, then D(G) = D∗ (G).
Theorem 2.3 (Geroldinger, [8, Corollary 4.2.13]) . If G′ = Ca ⊕ G, where G is
a p-group with D(G) ≤ 2 exp(G) − 1 and a ∈ N+, then D(G′) = D∗ (G′).
We will need the following result on short zero-sum subseque nces of zero-sum
sequences.
3
Theorem 2.4 (Fan, Gao, Wang, Zhong, Zhuang, [2, Theorem 2]) . If G =
Cmpn ⊕ H for some m, n ≥ 1, where H is a p-group with D(H) ≤ pn, then any
zero-sum sequence over G of length at least D(G) + 1 contains a short zero-sum
subsequence.
We are particularly interested in the case m = 1.
Corollary 2.5. If G is a p-group and D(G) ≤ 2 exp(G) − 1, then any zero-
sum sequence over G of length at least D(G) + 1 contains a short zero-sum
subsequence.
Proof. A p-group G with D(G) ≤ 2 exp(G) − 1 can be written in the form G =
Cpn ⊕ H, where pn = exp(G) and, by Theorem 2.2, D(H) = D(G)− (pn− 1) ≤ pn.
So, the conclusion of Theorem 2.4 holds for such p-groups G.
Finally, we will use the following tools to prove the extensi on of our main
result.
Lemma 2.6 (Edel, Elsholtz, Geroldinger, Kubertin, Rackham, [1, Lemm a 3.2]).
Let H be a ﬁnite abelian group and G′ = Cn ⊕ H, where exp(H) |n. Then
η(G′) ≥ 2(D(H) − 1) + n.
Proposition 2.7 (Geroldinger, Halter-Koch, [7, Proposition 5.7.11]) . If H is
a subgroup of G such that exp(G) = exp( H) exp(G/H ), then
η(G) ≤ (η(H) − 1) exp(G/H ) + η(G/H ).
Proposition 2.7 is an example of an application of the inductive method [7],
an approach used to obtain bounds on the zero-sum invariants of a group G in
terms of the zero-sum invariants of its subgroups.
3 Proof of the Main Result
3.1 The Invariants ζi
As the ﬁrst step in the proof of Theorem 1.6, we deﬁne the invar iants ζi(G) and
compute them for a p-group G.
Deﬁnition 2. Let G be a ﬁnite abelian group and let n = exp(G). For 1 ≤ i ≤
n, let
ζi(G) = s[i− n,0]+nN+(G).
That is, ζi(G) is the shortest length ℓ for which any sequence over G of
length at least ℓ contains a nonempty zero-sum subsequence T such that T is
congruent mod exp(G) to some residue between i and exp(G), inclusive.
Note that ζ1(G) = D(G) by deﬁnition, and ζexp(G) = sexp(G)N+ (G). When
G is a p-group, the precise value of sexp(G)N+(G) is known.
4
Lemma 3.1 (Gao, Geroldinger, [4, Theorem 6.7]) . If G is a p-group with n =
exp(G), then
snN+ (G) = D(G) + n − 1.
This allows us to compute ζi(G) for all i ∈ [1, n ] for a p-group G.
Lemma 3.2. Let G be a p-group and let n = exp(G). For 1 ≤ i ≤ n, we have
ζi(G) = D(G) + i − 1.
Proof. Let 1 ≤ i ≤ n − 1. For any t > 1 and sequence S, if the sequence 0S
obtained by prepending 0 to S contains a zero-sum subsequence of length t, then
S contains a zero-sum sequence of length t − 1 or t. So ζi(G) ≤ ζi+1(G) − 1.
Thus, by Lemma 3.1,
D(G) = ζ1(G) < ζ2(G) < · · ·< ζn(G) = D(G) + n − 1,
implying that ζi(G) = D(G) + i − 1 for each i, as desired.
Now we can proceed with the proof of Theorem 1.6 itself.
3.2 Main Proof
Proof of Theorem 1.6. Let G be a p-group with D(G) ≤ 2 exp(G) − 1. Let
n = exp( G). By Theorem 1.2, η(G) ≥ 2D(G) − n, so it suﬃces to show that
η(G) ≤ 2D(G) − n.
Let S be a sequence of length 2D(G) − n over G. We wish to show that S
contains a short zero-sum subsequence. Note that 1 ≤ D(G) − n + 1 ≤ n, so
by Lemma 3.2, |S| = ζD(G)− n+1(G). Thus, S contains a nonempty zero-sum
subsequence T whose length t is congruent mod n to some residue between
D(G) − n + 1 and n, inclusive. Then either t ≤ n or t ≥ D(G) + 1.
If t ≤ n, then T ⊆ S is a short zero-sum subsequence of S, as desired.
Otherwise, t ≥ D(G) + 1, so by Corollary 2.5, T contains a short zero-sum
subsequence, meaning S contains a short zero-sum subsequence. So S contains
a short zero-sum subsequence in any case.
Thus, η(G) = 2 D(G) − n, which rearranges to the desired equality.
4 Extension to Ca ⊕ G
As with Theorem 1.4, our main result can be extended to groups of the form
G′ = Ca⊕ G, where G is a p-group with D(G) ≤ 2 exp(G)− 1 and a is not divisible
by p. The proof is essentially the same as the derivation of Theor em 1.4 from
the a = 1 case, given in [5].
Theorem 4.1. Let G be a ﬁnite abelian p-group with D(G) ≤ 2 exp(G) − 1.
Then for any positive integer a not divisible by p, G′ = Ca ⊕ G satisﬁes
2D(G′) − 1 = η(G′) + exp(G′) − 1.
5
Proof. Let n = exp( G). By Theorem 2.3 we have D(G′) = D∗ (G′) = D(G) +
(a − 1)n, and by Lemma 2.6 we have η(G′) ≥ 2(D(G) − n) + an = 2D(G′) − an.
By Proposition 2.7, we have
η(G′) ≤ (η(Ca) − 1) exp(G) + η(G) = ( a − 1)n + η(G).
Finally, by Theorem 1.6, we have η(G) = 2 D(G) − n, so
η(G′) ≤ 2D(G) + (a − 2)n = 2D(G′) − an.
Thus η(G′) = 2 D(G′) − exp(G′), which rearranges to the desired equality.
Note that Theorem 4.1 is an extension of Theorem 4.2.1 in [6]. Remark 4.3
in [6] pointed out that a result like this would follow given T heorem 1.6.
5 T owards Evaluating s(G)
It would be interesting to see if a similar method suﬃces for v erifying the second
half of Conjecture 1.3, showing that s(G) = 2 D(G) − 1 for G a p-group with
D(G) ≤ 2 exp(G) − 1. Here we describe an approach that seems promising and
use it to make some progress.
Let n = exp( G). For 1 ≤ i ≤ n, let ηi(G) = s[i,n](G). Since a sequence S
contains a zero-sum subsequence of length i or i + 1 if 0S contains a zero-sum
subsequence of length i + 1, it is easy to see that
η(G) = η1(G) < · · ·< η n(G) = s(G).
So, if Conjecture 1.5 holds in general, ηi(G) = ηi− 1(G) + 1 = η(G) + i − 1
should always hold for 2 ≤ i ≤ n.

2 ⌋ + 1. However, we can apply the same idea
to get an improved result when specializing to a p-group of large exponent, via
a result generalizing Corollary 2.5 on short zero-sum subse quences of zero-sum

Theorem 5.2. Let G be a p-group with D(G) ≤ 2 exp(G) − 1. For 1 ≤ i ≤
2 exp(G) − D(G), any zero-sum sequence S of length exactly D(G) + i over G
contains a short zero-sum subsequence of length at least i.
Proof. We use strong induction on i. The base case i = 1 is Corollary 2.5.
Assume the result is true for 1 ≤ i ≤ j − 1, and take i = j ≤ 2 exp(G) − D(G).
Let n = exp(G) and consider a zero-sum sequence S of length D(G) + j over
G. Let S′ be the subsequence of S consisting of all but the last element, so S′

has length |S′|= D(G) +j − 1. By Lemma 3.2, S′ contains a zero-sum sequence
T whose length |T | is congruent mod n to some residue between j and n,
inclusive. Since D(G) + j ≤ 2n, either j ≤ | T | ≤ n or n + j ≤ | T | ≤ 2n − 1. In
the former case, T is a short zero-sum subsequence of S of length at least j, as
desired. In the latter case, the complement T ′ of T in S is a nonempty zero-sum
subsequence of S of length |T ′| ≤ (D(G) + j) − (n + j) = D(G) − n ≤ n − j.
If |T ′| ≥ j, we again have a short zero-sum subsequence of S of length at
least j, as desired. Otherwise, |T |= D(G) + j − |T ′|> D(G). By the inductive
hypothesis, T contains a short zero-sum subsequence U of length at least j −| T ′|.
If |U | ≥ j, we are again done, so assume j − |T ′| ≤ |U | ≤ j − 1. Then
j ≤ | T ′|+ |U | ≤ n − 1,
so T ′U is a short zero-sum subsequence of S of length at least j, as desired.
So the result holds for i = j as well, and thus by induction holds for all i ≤
2 exp(G) − D(G).

The argument used in Theorem 3.1 of [10] to prove that s(G) ≤ D(G)+2 n− 2
for a p-group G of large exponent relies on the fact that a sequence S over G of
length at least D(G)+(n− 2)+ℓ contains either a zero-sum subsequence of length
n, or a zero-sum subsequence of length 2n that itself contains a short zero-sum
subsequence of length at least ℓ. Our Theorem 5.2 provides a strengthening
of this result for ℓ = 1 , giving a short zero-sum subsequence of length at least
(2n − 1) − D(G) + ℓ = 2n − D(G) in the zero-sum subsequence of length 2n. It
is conceivable that this strengthening could generalize to larger values of ℓ, as
the following conjecture voices.
Conjecture 5.4. Let G be a ﬁnite abelian p-group with exp(G) = n and D(G) ≤
2n − 1. Let ℓ ∈ [1, D(G) + 1 − n], and let S be a sequence over G of length at
least D(G) + (n − 2) + ℓ. Then one of the following statements holds:

1. S has a zero-sum subsequence B with |B|= n.
2. S has a zero-sum subsequence B with |B|= 2n such that B has a zero-sum
subsequence B′ with |B′| ∈ [(2n − 1) − D(G) + ℓ, n − 1].
This conjecture, if true, would yield the desired bound of s(G) ≤ D(G) +
(n − 2) + (D(G) − n + 1) = 2 D(G) − 1. Perhaps in this or some other way, the
argument used in [10], extended by the arguments we use, coul d lead to the full
resolution of Conjecture 1.3.
